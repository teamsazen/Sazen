The 99 Names of Allah, known as Asmaul Husna ("the most beautiful names"), describe who Allah is: His mercy, His knowledge, His power and His care for His creation. Learning them is one of the most direct ways to know Him better, and each Name gives you a new way to call upon Him in dua.

## Why the Names matter

The Quran itself points us to them:

> "And to Allah belong the most beautiful names, so call upon Him by them." (Al-A'raf 7:180)

A well-known hadith, reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, says that Allah has ninety-nine names and that whoever learns and preserves them will enter Paradise. Scholars explain that this means more than memorising a list: knowing what each Name means, believing in it, and letting it shape how you pray and live.

## The order of the Names

The list most people learn, and the one below, follows the order narrated by at-Tirmidhi. It begins with Ar-Rahman and Ar-Raheem, the two Names that also open every surah but one in the Bismillah, and ends with As-Sabur, The Most Patient.

## How to learn them

Ninety-nine is a lot to learn at once, and you do not have to. A few habits make it easier:

1. **Learn three a day.** In just over a month you will have met every Name. Read the Arabic, say the transliteration out loud and learn the meaning.
2. **Read the reflection, not only the word.** "The Most Gracious" stays with you longer when you think about what His grace means in your own day.
3. **Use them in dua.** Ask for mercy with Ar-Rahman, for provision with Ar-Razzaq, for forgiveness with Al-Ghaffar. The Names turn into words you actually use.
4. **Come back to your favourites.** Some Names will speak to you more than others at different times. Keep a short list and return to it.
5. **Teach them.** Explaining a Name to a child or a friend is one of the best ways to remember it yourself.
