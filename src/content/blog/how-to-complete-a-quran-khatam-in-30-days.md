Finishing the whole Quran, a khatam, feels like a big goal until you see it as a daily habit. The Quran has 114 surahs and 6,236 ayahs, usually divided into 30 juz (parts). Read one juz a day and you finish in a month. This guide turns that into a plan you can actually keep, whether you are aiming for Ramadan or for any month of the year.

## How much to read each day

In the common 15-line mushaf, the Quran runs to about 604 pages. Spread across 30 days, that is roughly:

| Goal | Each day | Pages a day | Ayahs a day (about) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One khatam in 30 days | 1 juz | about 20 | about 208 |
| One khatam in 15 days | 2 juz | about 40 | about 416 |
| One khatam in 60 days | half a juz | about 10 | about 104 |
| Two khatams in Ramadan | 2 juz | about 40 | about 416 |

The ayah counts are averages: some juz have far more ayahs than others, because ayah length changes a lot through the Quran. Pages are a steadier guide to how long a day's reading takes.

## Split it around salah

Twenty pages in one sitting is hard for most people. Five short sittings are not. Tie your reading to the five daily prayers:

- **After Fajr:** 4 pages
- **After Zuhr:** 4 pages
- **After Asr:** 4 pages
- **After Maghrib:** 4 pages
- **After Isha:** 4 pages

Four pages takes most readers 10 to 15 minutes. Because salah already sets the rhythm of the day, you never have to decide when to read. If one sitting gets missed, add those pages to the next one rather than leaving them for the weekend.

## Pick a start date and count backwards

For Ramadan, start on the first night or the first day and read one juz a day. Ramadan lasts 29 or 30 days, so keep a little margin: reading slightly more in the first week, for example 22 pages a day, leaves room for a busy day near the end.

Outside Ramadan, any month works the same way. Write the date you want to finish, count the days, and divide the 604 pages by them. A 45-day plan is about 13 or 14 pages a day; a 90-day plan is under 7.

## When you miss a day

Everyone misses a day. What matters is having a rule before it happens:

1. **Do not restart.** Your progress is not lost; you are simply behind.
2. **Spread the gap.** If you missed 20 pages, add 4 to each of the next five days instead of reading 40 tomorrow.
3. **Move the finish line if you need to.** Finishing on day 33 is still a khatam.

## Read with understanding

A khatam is not only about the last page. Keeping a translation open next to the Arabic, even for a few ayahs in each sitting, makes the reading stay with you. Some people read the Arabic for the main plan and spend their last sitting of the day on the translation of what they read.

Listening helps too. Following a recitation while you read the mushaf keeps your pace steady and is especially useful on tired days.

## Keep track of where you are

The hardest part of a long plan is losing your place, especially when you read in different places: a mushaf at the masjid, a phone on the way to work, a book at home. A simple record of what you have finished keeps the plan honest and shows you how far you have come.

## A plan you can start tonight

1. Choose your finish date.
2. Divide 604 pages by the number of days.
3. Split each day's pages across your five prayers.
4. Keep a record of every surah you finish.
5. When you miss a day, spread the gap instead of starting over.
